## Matter

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slide 3</p>

- **Matter** is anything that has mass and occupies space.
- **Weight is not mass.** Weight is the pull of gravity on a mass:

<div class="formula">W = m × g      SI: newtons = kilograms × 9.81 m/s²
                 imperial: pounds-force = slugs × 32.2 ft/s²</div>

- States of matter: solid, liquid, gas, plasma. For the technician only the first three matter.

## The atom and its three particles

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slides 4–6</p>

| Particle | Charge | Where | Mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proton | + | nucleus | ≈ 1836 × electron |
| Neutron | 0 | nucleus | ≈ proton |
| Electron | − | shells around the nucleus | very small |

- The **mass** of an atom is set by its protons and neutrons (the nucleus).
- The **chemistry and electricity** of an atom are set by its electrons, because they are on the outside.
- The **number of protons** (atomic number) decides which element an atom is. More or fewer neutrons give an **isotope** of the same element; more or fewer electrons give an **ion** of the same element.

## Neutral atoms: hydrogen and oxygen

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slide 7</p>

A **neutral** atom has as many electrons as protons, so the charges cancel.

| Atom | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons | Shells |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrogen | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| Oxygen | 8 | 8 | 8 | 2 (2 + 6) |

## Elements, compounds, molecules

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slide 8</p>

- **Element**: cannot be split into simpler substances by chemical means (Fe, Cu, Au, O).
- **Compound**: two or more elements chemically combined (water, H₂O).
- **Molecule**: the smallest particle that is still that substance. Split a water molecule and you have hydrogen and oxygen, not water.

## Shells and energy levels

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slides 9–11</p>

Electrons sit only at certain energy levels, grouped into **shells**. The most a shell can hold is:

<div class="formula">Electron capacity = 2n²      n = 1, 2, 3, 4  →  2, 8, 18, 32</div>

- Shells fill from the inside (lowest energy) outwards, but a shell need not be full before the next one starts.
- The heaviest atoms have **seven** shells. The periodic table gives each element's configuration.

## Valence, ions, free electrons

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slide 12</p>

- **Valence electrons**: the electrons in the outermost shell. They take part in bonding.
- **Ionization**: gaining or losing electrons.
  - Loses an electron → **positive ion (cation)**.
  - Gains an electron → **negative ion (anion)**.
- **Free electrons**: loosely bound outer electrons that leave their atom and drift from atom to atom. Their flow is the **current** in a circuit.

## Conductors, insulators, semiconductors

<p class="ref">Notes 3.01, slides 13–14</p>

| Type | Free electrons | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conductor | many (one loose valence electron) | silver, copper, gold, aluminium |
| Insulator | almost none | glass, ceramic, plastic, rubber |
| Semiconductor | in between, depends on conditions | silicon, germanium |

- The more free electrons, the more current a material can carry.
- Heat can free outer electrons in a conductor.
- An insulator in a very strong electric field can have its electrons torn loose. This is **breakdown**, and it usually damages the insulator.
- Semiconductors are the material of transistors and chips (Module 4).

> Mass from the nucleus, electricity from the outer shell.
> Protons decide the element.
> 2n²: 2, 8, 18, 32.
> Lose an electron → positive ion.

## Corrections to the course notes

- **Slide 4** says an element is defined by its numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons. Only the **protons** define it (neutrons → isotopes, electrons → ions). It also says hydrogen has neutrons: ordinary hydrogen has **none**.
- **Slide 6** gives the proton/electron mass ratio as both 1 837 and 1 845. Use **≈ 1836**. It also says it is "not certain" whether the electron has mass. It does: 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.
- **Slide 10**: "the seventh shell … which have six energy levels" is garbled. The heaviest atoms have **seven shells**.
