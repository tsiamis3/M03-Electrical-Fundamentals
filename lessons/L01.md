# Lesson 1 — Introduction and electron theory

Module 3 covers how electricity is made, stored, carried and used on an aircraft. Today we start with the atom: everything electrical, from a static spark to a 115 V generator, is electrons moving.

## Matter

- **Matter** is anything that has mass and occupies space.
- **Weight is not mass.** Weight is the pull of gravity on a mass:

<div class="formula">W = m × g      (newtons = kilograms × m/s²)</div>

- States of matter: solid, liquid, gas (and plasma). For the technician only the first three matter.

## The atom

Three particles decide everything about an atom:

| Particle | Charge | Where | Mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proton | + | nucleus | ≈ 1836 × electron |
| Neutron | 0 | nucleus | ≈ proton |
| Electron | − | shells around the nucleus | very small |

- The **mass** of an atom is set by its protons and neutrons.
- The **chemistry and electricity** of an atom are set by its electrons, because they are on the outside.
- A **neutral** atom has as many electrons as protons. Hydrogen: 1 proton, 1 electron. Oxygen: 8 protons, 8 neutrons, 8 electrons in two shells.

**Element**: one kind of atom (Fe, Cu, O). **Compound**: two or more elements combined (H₂O). **Molecule**: the smallest particle that is still that substance.

## Shells and energy levels

Electrons sit only at certain energy levels, grouped into shells. The most a shell can hold is:

<div class="formula">Electron capacity = 2n²      n = 1, 2, 3 …  →  2, 8, 18, 32</div>

Shells fill from the inside out, though a shell need not be full before the next one starts. The heaviest atoms have seven shells.

- **Valence electrons**: the electrons in the outermost shell. They take part in bonding.
- **Ionization**: gaining or losing electrons. Lose one → **positive ion (cation)**. Gain one → **negative ion (anion)**.
- **Free electrons**: loosely bound outer electrons that drift from atom to atom. Their flow is the current in a circuit.

## Conductors, insulators, semiconductors

| Type | Free electrons | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conductor | many (one loose valence electron) | silver, copper, gold, aluminium |
| Insulator | almost none | glass, ceramic, plastic |
| Semiconductor | in between, depends on conditions | silicon, germanium |

An insulator in a very strong electric field can have its electrons torn loose. This is **breakdown**, and it usually damages the insulator.

> Remember: mass from the nucleus, electricity from the outer shell.
> 2n² gives 2, 8, 18, 32.
> Lose an electron → positive ion.
