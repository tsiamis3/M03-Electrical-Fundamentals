# Lesson 4 — Generation of electricity + quiz A

<p class="ref">Course notes: <b>3.04 Generation of Electricity</b>, slides 1–9</p>

Where does the push (emf) come from? Every source of electricity turns some other form of energy into electrical energy. There are seven ways, and an aircraft uses almost all of them. In the second hour: **quiz A** on lessons 1–4.

## The seven sources at a glance

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slide 2</p>

| Energy in | Device | On the aircraft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | photovoltaic (solar) cell | light sensors, satellites |
| Heat | thermocouple | EGT, ITT, CHT, fire detection |
| Friction | — (static electricity) | static on the airframe and in fuel |
| Pressure | piezoelectric crystal | vibration sensors, microphones, buzzers |
| Chemical action | cell, battery | main and emergency batteries |
| Magnetism + motion | generator | engine-driven generators and alternators |

## Light: photovoltaic cells

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slide 3</p>

- A **solar (photovoltaic) cell** turns light into electricity.
- **Photons** are absorbed by a **semiconductor** such as silicon. Each absorbed photon can free an electron from its atom.
- The empty place it leaves behind is a **hole**, which behaves like a positive charge. Electrons and holes flow in **opposite directions**, and together they give the current.
- Uses: satellites and space probes, calculators, watches, light sensors.

## Heat: thermocouples

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slides 4–5</p>

- A **thermocouple** is two **different** metal wires joined at a junction.
- Heat the junction and a small voltage appears between the wires: the **Seebeck effect** (Thomas Seebeck, 1821).
- The voltage depends on the **temperature difference** between the hot junction and the cold (reference) end of the wires, and on the pair of metals.
- **Same temperature everywhere → no voltage.** Same metal on both sides → no voltage either.
- Cheap, interchangeable, wide temperature range. On aircraft: **cylinder head temperature (CHT)**, **inter-turbine temperature (ITT)** and **exhaust gas temperature (EGT)**.

## Friction

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slide 5</p>

Rubbing two non-conducting materials transfers electrons: static electricity (lesson 2). It is a nuisance, not a useful source, on an aircraft.

## Pressure: piezoelectricity

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slides 6–7</p>

- Squeeze certain **crystals** (non-conductors) and a voltage appears across them, **proportional to the stress**. *Piezo* is from the Greek πιέζω, to press.
- Materials: **quartz**, **Rochelle salt**, and newer ceramics such as **barium titanate**.
- It works **both ways**:

| Direction | Uses |
| --- | --- |
| pressure → voltage | microphones, pickups, **vibration sensors** (engine vibration monitoring) |
| voltage → movement | buzzers, small speakers and headphones, ultrasonic transducers |

## Chemical action: cells and batteries

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slide 8</p>

- A **cell** has two different electrodes in an **electrolyte**. A chemical reaction moves electrons from one electrode to the other.
- In an alkaline cell the electrodes are **zinc** and **manganese dioxide**. Zinc releases electrons: it is the **negative (−)** terminal. The other electrode is **positive (+)**.
- Connect a wire: electrons flow through it from − to +, until the chemicals are used up. Details in lessons 5 and 6.

## Magnetism and motion

<p class="ref">Notes 3.04, slide 9</p>

Move a conductor through a **magnetic field** (or move the field past the conductor) and an **emf** appears in it. Every aircraft generator works like this. More in magnetism (3.10) and induction (3.11).

> Light, heat, friction, pressure, chemical, magnetism + motion.
> Thermocouple: two different metals, a temperature **difference**.
> Piezo works both ways.
> The electrode that releases electrons is −.

## Corrections to the course notes

- **Slide 4**: the thermocouple voltage depends on the **temperature difference** between the two junctions, not on the temperature of one junction. Slide 5 says the same thing the other way round: no difference, no voltage.
- **Slide 8**: the electrode that releases more electrons (zinc) becomes **negative**, not positive. It keeps the extra electrons and sends them out through the wire. Manganese dioxide is a metal **oxide**, not a metal.
- **Slide 7**: most loudspeakers are electromagnetic (a coil and a magnet). Piezoelectric ones are buzzers, small speakers and tweeters.
