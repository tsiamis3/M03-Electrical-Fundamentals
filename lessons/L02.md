# Lesson 2 — Static electricity and conduction

Yesterday: electricity is electrons. Today: what happens when electrons pile up and stay put (static), how charges push and pull on each other (Coulomb), and how charge actually moves through solids, liquids, gases and vacuum.

## Static and dynamic electricity

| | Static | Dynamic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electrons | at rest, piled up on a surface | moving (a current) |
| Typical cause | rubbing two insulators together | a battery or generator in a closed circuit |
| How you notice it | attraction/repulsion, a spark | lamps, motors, heat |

A static charge appears whenever two surfaces **touch and separate** and at least one of them is an **insulator**. The insulator cannot let the extra (or missing) electrons flow away, so the imbalance stays. A **spark** is that imbalance being neutralized through the air to a conductor or to ground.

**Lightning** is the same thing scaled up: charge separated by collisions of ice particles inside a storm cloud. The flash is air heated so much that it glows. The thunder is the shock wave from that air expanding.

## The triboelectric effect

When two materials touch, electrons cross from one to the other:

- the material with **loosely held** electrons loses them → becomes **positive**;
- the material that **holds electrons more strongly** gains them → becomes **negative**.

Rubbing just makes many more contacts. The total charge is conserved: one gains exactly what the other loses.

| Rubbed together | Becomes positive | Becomes negative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glass rod and silk | glass | silk |
| Plastic (ebonite) rod and fur | fur | plastic |

## Attraction and repulsion

<div class="formula">Like charges repel.      Unlike charges attract.</div>

- Two pith balls touched by the same charged rod get the same charge and **repel**.
- One touched by a glass rod (+), one by a plastic rod (−): opposite charges, they **attract**.
- A charged object also attracts a **neutral** one. It does this by separating the charges inside it (induction, below). Styrofoam on a cat's fur and static cling in clothes are this effect.

## Electric field and field lines

A charged body has an **electric (electrostatic) field** around it: a region where another charge feels a force.

- Field lines show the **direction** a small **positive test charge** would be pushed.
- Lines go **out of positive** charges and **into negative** charges. They start and end on charges.
- **Density of lines = strength of the field.** Lines never cross.
- Between two like charges, the lines bend away from each other: there is a point between them with no field.

## How charge sits on a conductor

Free electrons in a conductor push each other as far apart as they can. The result:

1. **All the excess charge goes to the outer surface.** The inside of a conductor carries no excess charge.
2. **A flat disc or a sphere**: the charge spreads evenly.
3. **A hollow sphere**: all the charge is on the outside. The inside is field-free. This is why the safest place in a Van de Graaff generator is inside the sphere, and why an aircraft skin protects what is inside it in a lightning strike.
4. **An irregular shape**: the charge concentrates at **points and sharp edges** (tightest curvature). The field there is strongest, so that is where the air breaks down first.

> Aircraft connection: **static wicks** on the trailing edges of wings and tail are made sharp on purpose. They let charge leak off into the air slowly at a chosen place, instead of discharging in bursts that cause radio noise.

## Charging by contact and by induction

**By contact:** a charged rod touches a neutral metal bar. Some charge moves across, so the bar ends up with the **same sign** as the rod, and the rod ends up weaker.

**By induction** (the rod never touches):

1. Bring a **+** rod near a neutral bar. Electrons in the bar are pulled to the near end; the far end is left **+**.
2. **Ground** the bar (touch it). Electrons flow in from ground and cancel the far end.
3. **Remove the ground first**, then remove the rod.
4. The bar is left with an excess of electrons: **opposite sign** to the rod.

| Method | Touches? | Sign of the charge left on the bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact | yes | same as the rod |
| Induction (with grounding) | no | opposite to the rod |

## Units of charge and Coulomb's law

- **Elementary charge** e: the charge of one proton (+e) or one electron (−e).
- **Coulomb (C)**: the SI unit of charge. One coulomb is the charge carried by a current of one ampere in one second.

<div class="formula">Q = I × t      1 C = 1 A × 1 s</div>

<div class="formula">e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C      1 C = 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons</div>

So a current of 1 A means 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons passing a point **every second**.

**Coulomb's law**: the force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.

<div class="formula">F = k × Q₁ × Q₂ / d²      k = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²</div>

| Change | Effect on the force |
| --- | --- |
| Double one charge | × 2 |
| Double both charges | × 4 |
| Double the distance | ÷ 4 |
| Halve the distance | × 4 |
| Triple the distance | ÷ 9 |

> The most common exam mistake: forgetting the **square** on the distance.

## Electrostatic discharge (ESD)

Your body can carry thousands of volts without you feeling it. Touch a sensitive component and the discharge can destroy it without any visible mark.

- Wear a **grounding wrist strap**, or touch the chassis of the removed unit first.
- Do **not** touch connector pins of LRUs and circuit cards. Fit **protective caps** on open connectors.
- Keep ESD-sensitive parts in their **antistatic bags**. No carpet-covered toolbox tops.
- Connect a **ground** between the test equipment and the circuit **before** injecting or monitoring a signal.
- Do not exceed the components' **maximum test voltages**. Do not test transistors on ohmmeter ranges that drive **more than 1 mA**.
- Solder with a **low-wattage iron** and a **heat sink**. Do not pry components off a board. **Remove power** before replacing a part.
- Keep exposed probe tips **short** where test points are close together.

### Refuelling

Fuel flowing through hoses and filters builds up static charge, because fuel is a poor conductor. A spark in the fuel vapour can cause an explosion. That is why the aircraft and the fuel truck are **bonded** together and **grounded** before refuelling starts.

## Conduction in solids, liquids, gases and vacuum

| Medium | What carries the current | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solids (metals) | free electrons | loosely held valence electrons; metals are excellent conductors |
| Liquid metals (mercury) | free electrons | same as solid metals |
| Other liquids (electrolytes) | **ions**, both + and − | water conducts because of dissolved salts that split into ions; very pure water is a poor conductor |
| Gases | ions and electrons | normally good insulators; a strong enough field ionizes the gas (spark, lightning, neon lamp) |
| Vacuum | electrons that are **injected** (e.g. from a heated cathode) | nothing there to carry current by itself, but nothing to slow injected electrons either (vacuum tubes, CRTs) |

> Remember: in a metal only electrons move; in a liquid or a gas both positive and negative ions move.

## Notes on the course deck

- Slide 14 says "an ampere represents the flow of 6.241 × 10¹⁸ electrons". It is **per second**: 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons is one **coulomb**; one ampere is one coulomb per second.
- Slide 17 says metals have valence electrons "with weak covalent bonds". In metals it is **metallic bonding**: the valence electrons are shared by the whole crystal. Just say "loosely held".
- Slide 17 calls the vacuum an "ideal conductor". Better: a vacuum has **no carriers of its own**; it conducts only electrons that are put into it.
- Slide 17 says water "ionizes when electricity is applied". The ions are already there (dissolved salts). Pure water hardly conducts.
- On the induction slide (16), the order matters: remove the **ground first**, then the rod. Otherwise the electrons flow back out.
- Typos in the deck: "THRIBOELECTRIC" → triboelectric; "uncharted" → uncharged; "lightening" → lightning.
